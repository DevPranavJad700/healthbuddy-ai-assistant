=== ASTHMA ===
Chronic inflammatory airway disease; episodic, reversible bronchoconstriction

Classic symptoms: Wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough (often worse at night/early morning)
Triggers: Allergens (dust mites, pollen, pets), exercise, cold air, smoke, infections (viral URTIs), NSAIDs (aspirin-exacerbated), stress, strong smells

Severity classification:
- Intermittent: Symptoms ≤2 days/week; nighttime ≤2x/month; no activity limitation
- Mild persistent: >2 days/week but not daily; nighttime 3–4x/month
- Moderate persistent: Daily symptoms; nighttime >1x/week
- Severe persistent: Continuous symptoms; frequent nighttime symptoms

Treatment (step-up approach):
Step 1: SABA (salbutamol/albuterol) PRN only — mild intermittent
Step 2: Add low-dose ICS (inhaled corticosteroid — budesonide, fluticasone)
Step 3: Add LABA (long-acting beta agonist — salmeterol, formoterol) to ICS
Step 4: Increase ICS dose; add LTRA (montelukast) or LAMA (tiotropium)
Step 5: Add biologic (mepolizumab, dupilumab) for severe eosinophilic asthma

Inhaler technique:
- MDI: Shake, exhale fully, seal lips around mouthpiece, inhale slowly over 5 sec while pressing, hold breath 10 sec, wait 30–60 sec between puffs
- Spacer device greatly improves lung deposition (especially in children)
- Dry powder inhalers (DPI): Breathe in quickly and deeply

Asthma attack management:
Mild: SABA 2–4 puffs every 20 min for 3 doses
Moderate: SABA + oral prednisolone 40mg
Severe: SABA + ipratropium + IV magnesium; call emergency services if: unable to speak, SpO2 <92%, silent chest

Control assessment: ACQ or ACT score; aim for symptom-free days, no reliever use, normal lung function

=== COPD (CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE) ===
Progressive, largely irreversible airflow limitation; caused by prolonged exposure to noxious particles/gases
Smoking cause: >85% of cases; biomass fuel exposure (developing world); occupational dusts

Symptoms: Progressive exertional dyspnoea, chronic cough (productive), frequent chest infections
Emphysema: Alveolar destruction → hyperinflation, air trapping, barrel chest
Chronic bronchitis: Productive cough ≥3 months/year for ≥2 consecutive years

Diagnosis: Spirometry — FEV1/FVC <0.70 post-bronchodilator
GOLD staging (by FEV1% predicted): Stage I (Mild, ≥80%) → II (Moderate, 50–79%) → III (Severe, 30–49%) → IV (Very Severe, <30%)

Treatment:
- Smoking cessation: Single most effective intervention; slows decline from 60mL/year to 30mL/year FEV1 loss
- Bronchodilators: SABA (salbutamol), SAMA (ipratropium), LABA (indacaterol), LAMA (tiotropium)
- ICS: For eosinophil count >300/µL or frequent exacerbations
- Pulmonary rehabilitation: Mandatory for MRC dyspnoea grade ≥3; improves exercise capacity, QoL
- Oxygen therapy: If PaO2 <55 mmHg or SpO2 <88% at rest → 15h/day LTOT
- Vaccination: Annual influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV vaccine (≥60 years)

COPD exacerbation: Acute worsening of dyspnoea, cough, sputum production
- Managed with: SABA, SAMA, systemic steroids (prednisolone 30–40mg x5 days), antibiotics if purulent sputum

=== PNEUMONIA ===
Infection of lung parenchyma (alveoli); bacterial, viral, or fungal

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) causes:
- Streptococcus pneumoniae (most common bacterial)
- Atypical: Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydophila, Legionella
- Viral: Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV

Symptoms: Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, tachypnoea
Consolidation on CXR (may be patchy in atypical pneumonia)

Severity (CURB-65 score — 1 point each):
- Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30, BP <90/60, Age ≥65
- Score 0–1: Treat at home
- Score 2: Hospital admission
- Score 3+: ICU consideration

Treatment:
- Low severity CAP: Amoxicillin 500mg TDS x5 days
- Atypical/penicillin allergy: Doxycycline or clarithromycin
- Hospitalised: Co-amoxiclav + macrolide or cefuroxime + doxycycline

=== UPPER RESPIRATORY TRACT INFECTIONS ===

Common Cold (rhinovirus, typically):
- Duration: 7–10 days; contagious days 1–3
- Treatment: Symptomatic only (saline nasal rinse, paracetamol, zinc lozenges may reduce duration by 1 day)
- Antibiotics: NOT indicated (viral); prescribing = antibiotic resistance

Influenza:
- Sudden onset high fever, severe myalgia, headache, dry cough; prostration (feeling "knocked down") distinguishes from cold
- Complications: Pneumonia, myocarditis, encephalitis; most serious in elderly, immunocompromised, pregnant
- Treatment: Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) — start within 48h; reduces duration by 1–2 days; most benefit in high-risk
- Prevention: Annual vaccination — 40–70% effective (varies by strain match)

COVID-19:
- Variants ongoing; current vaccines provide good protection against severe disease
- Symptoms range: Asymptomatic → fever, cough, fatigue, dyspnoea → severe pneumonia, ARDS
- Antivirals: Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid) — high-risk patients within 5 days of symptom onset
- Long COVID: Symptoms persisting >4 weeks; fatigue, brain fog, dyspnoea common; multidisciplinary management

=== PULMONARY EMBOLISM (PE) — URGENT ===
Blood clot in pulmonary artery — medical emergency

Risk factors: DVT, immobilisation, surgery, cancer, OCP/HRT, long flights, pregnancy
Symptoms: Sudden pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, tachycardia, haemoptysis, syncope
Massive PE: Haemodynamic instability (shock, hypotension) → immediate thrombolysis

Diagnosis: D-dimer (sensitive, not specific), CT pulmonary angiography (gold standard)
Treatment: Anticoagulation (DOAC or heparin bridge to warfarin); massive PE → thrombolysis

Any sudden severe unexplained dyspnoea or pleuritic chest pain → emergency evaluation
