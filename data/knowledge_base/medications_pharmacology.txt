=== PAIN RELIEF (ANALGESICS) ===

Paracetamol / Acetaminophen (Tylenol):
- Uses: Mild-moderate pain, fever
- Safe dose: Adults — up to 1g per dose, max 4g/day (3g/day if alcohol use)
- Overdose risk: Liver damage (even slight overdose). Never exceed the daily limit.
- Interactions: Warfarin (enhanced effect), heavy alcohol (liver toxicity)
- Safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding at recommended doses

NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac):
- Uses: Inflammatory pain, musculoskeletal pain, menstrual cramps, fever
- Take with food to reduce GI upset
- Avoid in: Peptic ulcer, CKD, heart failure, late pregnancy, before surgery
- Side effects: GI bleeding, fluid retention, raised blood pressure, kidney impairment
- COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib): lower GI risk but same cardiovascular risk as NSAIDs
- Interactions: Blood thinners (bleeding risk), ACE inhibitors (reduced efficacy), diuretics

Opioids (Codeine, Tramadol, Morphine, Oxycodone):
- Uses: Moderate-severe pain (acute or cancer-related); chronic pain only when other options exhausted
- Risk: Physical dependence, respiratory depression, constipation, sedation
- Never combine with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or other CNS depressants (risk of fatal respiratory depression)
- Tramadol: also an SNRI — risk of serotonin syndrome; lowers seizure threshold

=== ANTIBIOTICS ===
General principles:
- Complete the full course even if symptoms improve (prevents resistance)
- Most upper respiratory infections (colds, flu) are viral — antibiotics DO NOT help
- Common side effects: GI upset, diarrhoea, C. difficile colitis with prolonged use
- Allergy: penicillin allergy (rash only) usually allows cephalosporins; anaphylaxis = avoid all beta-lactams

Amoxicillin: Strep throat, ear infections, dental infections. Take with food.
Azithromycin: Atypical pneumonia, STIs, traveller's diarrhoea. QT prolongation risk.
Ciprofloxacin: UTI, GI infections. Avoid with antacids. Tendon rupture risk with steroids.
Metronidazole: C. diff, bacterial vaginosis, H. pylori. Severe alcohol interaction (disulfiram-like reaction — avoid alcohol completely).
Doxycycline: Malaria prophylaxis, Lyme disease, acne, atypical pneumonia. Photosensitivity — use sunscreen. Take with food.

=== BLOOD THINNERS (ANTICOAGULANTS) ===

Warfarin:
- Requires INR monitoring (target range 2.0–3.0 for most indications)
- Many drug and food interactions: Vitamin K-rich foods (leafy greens) reduce effect; NSAIDs, antibiotics may increase INR
- Signs of over-anticoagulation: Unusual bruising, blood in urine/stool, prolonged bleeding

DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban, Dabigatran, Edoxaban):
- No routine monitoring needed
- More predictable than warfarin, fewer food interactions
- Rivaroxaban: take with food for full absorption
- Dabigatran: antidote available (idarucizumab)
- Do NOT miss doses or double up missed doses

Aspirin (antiplatelet):
- 75–100mg/day for secondary prevention (post-MI, stroke, CAD)
- Primary prevention: benefits outweigh risk only in specific high-risk patients

=== DIABETES MEDICATIONS ===
Metformin:
- Hold 48h before IV contrast (risk of lactic acidosis)
- Take with food to reduce GI side effects
- Safe, weight-neutral, first-line

Insulin:
- Keep refrigerated; discard vials open >28–30 days
- Never skip insulin if sick (illness increases glucose needs)
- Rotate injection sites to prevent lipohypertrophy

SGLT2 inhibitors:
- Genital mycotic infections common (especially in women)
- Hold before major surgery (risk of euglycaemic DKA)
- Drink adequate water to prevent UTIs

=== MEDICATIONS TO AVOID IN SPECIFIC CONDITIONS ===

In Pregnancy (Category D/X):
- NSAIDs (third trimester): premature ductus closure
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs: teratogenic (foetal renal damage)
- Warfarin (first trimester): foetal warfarin syndrome
- Tetracyclines/doxycycline: foetal bone/teeth effects
- Statins: teratogenic
- Methotrexate: highly teratogenic

In Renal Impairment (CKD stage 3+):
- Metformin: hold if eGFR < 30
- NSAIDs: worsen kidney function, avoid
- Gabapentin/pregabalin: reduce dose
- DOAC dosing adjustment required

In Elderly (Beers Criteria — avoid):
- Benzodiazepines: fall risk, cognitive impairment
- First-generation antihistamines (diphenhydramine): anticholinergic — confusion, urinary retention
- Muscle relaxants (cyclobenzaprine): excessive sedation
- Digoxin > 0.125mg: toxicity risk

=== DANGEROUS DRUG INTERACTIONS ===
1. Warfarin + NSAIDs = major bleeding risk
2. Statins + clarithromycin/erythromycin = rhabdomyolysis
3. SSRIs + tramadol = serotonin syndrome
4. MAOIs + any serotonergic drug = serotonin syndrome (can be fatal)
5. Metronidazole + alcohol = severe nausea/vomiting/flushing
6. Quinolones + antacids = absorption failure (space 2 hours apart)
7. Opioids + benzodiazepines = respiratory depression (leading cause of overdose deaths)
