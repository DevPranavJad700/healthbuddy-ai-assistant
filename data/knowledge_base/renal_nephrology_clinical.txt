=== CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDE: RENAL & NEPHROLOGY HEALTH ===
Category: Nephrology / Kidney Health
Target Audience: Clinical Triage, Primary Care Clinicians, Patient Health Guidance
Evidence Base: KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes), KDOQI, American Society of Nephrology (ASN)

1. OVERVIEW OF RENAL PHYSIOLOGY & FUNCTION
The kidneys filter approximately 120 to 150 quarts of blood daily, eliminating metabolic waste products (urea, creatinine, uric acid), regulating extracellular fluid volume, maintaining acid-base and electrolyte equilibrium (potassium, sodium, bicarbonate, phosphate, calcium), and producing vital hormones (erythropoietin for red cell production, calcitriol [active vitamin D] for calcium balance, and renin for systemic blood pressure regulation).

2. CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD) STAGING & MONITORING
Chronic Kidney Disease is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for > 3 months with health implications.
Diagnosis requires evidence of kidney damage (typically persistent albuminuria) or decreased glomerular filtration rate (GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m2).

CKD Staging by Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR, mL/min/1.73 m2):
- Stage G1: Normal or high (eGFR >= 90) with confirmed kidney damage (proteinuria/hematuria).
- Stage G2: Mildly decreased (eGFR 60 - 89) with confirmed kidney damage.
- Stage G3a: Mildly to moderately decreased (eGFR 45 - 59).
- Stage G3b: Moderately to severely decreased (eGFR 30 - 44).
- Stage G4: Severely decreased (eGFR 15 - 29). Preparation for renal replacement therapy/transplant.
- Stage G5: Kidney Failure / End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) (eGFR < 15). Dialysis or kidney transplantation required.

Albuminuria Staging (Urinary Albumin-to-Creatinine Ratio, UACR):
- Category A1 (Normal to mildly increased): UACR < 30 mg/g (< 3 mg/mmol).
- Category A2 (Moderately increased / Microalbuminuria): UACR 30 - 300 mg/g (3 - 30 mg/mmol). Early marker of vascular and renal injury.
- Category A3 (Severely increased / Macroalbuminuria): UACR > 300 mg/g (> 30 mg/mmol). High risk of CKD progression and cardiovascular mortality.

3. DIABETIC NEPHROPATHY & HYPERTENSIVE NEPHROSCLEROSIS
Diabetes and hypertension account for over 70% of end-stage renal disease cases globally.
Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT):
- Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitors: First-line therapy for patients with hypertension and albuminuria (UACR >= 30 mg/g). ACE inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril 10-40 mg daily) or Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs, e.g., Losartan 50-100 mg daily). Monitor serum creatinine and potassium 1-2 weeks after initiation; up to a 30% rise in creatinine is acceptable and reflects intraglomerular hemodynamic changes.
- SGLT2 Inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin): Class 1A recommendation for patients with CKD and type 2 diabetes or nondiabetic CKD with eGFR >= 20 mL/min/1.73 m2. Dramatically slows eGFR decline, reduces proteinuria, and lowers heart failure hospitalization.
- Non-steroidal Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (Finerenone): Indicated for persistent albuminuria despite maximally tolerated ACEi/ARB therapy in type 2 diabetes. Reduces renal and cardiovascular endpoints while monitoring serum potassium closely.
- Blood Pressure Targets: Target systolic blood pressure < 120 mmHg when tolerated, measured using standardized office BP techniques (KDIGO 2021).

4. ACUTE KIDNEY INJURY (AKI) KDIGO DEFINITIONS & PHASES
AKI is characterized by an abrupt decline in renal function occurring over hours to days.
KDIGO Staging Criteria:
- Stage 1: Serum creatinine 1.5 - 1.9 times baseline OR increase >= 0.3 mg/dL (>= 26.5 umol/L) within 48 hours; urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 - 12 hours.
- Stage 2: Serum creatinine 2.0 - 2.9 times baseline; urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for >= 12 hours.
- Stage 3: Serum creatinine 3.0 times baseline OR increase >= 4.0 mg/dL (>= 353.6 umol/L) OR initiation of renal replacement therapy; urine output < 0.3 mL/kg/h for >= 24 hours or anuria for >= 12 hours.

Etiological Classification:
- Prerenal Azotemia: Volume depletion, dehydration, sepsis, hemorrhage, cardiac failure, bilateral renal artery stenosis. BUN:Creatinine ratio often > 20:1, fractional excretion of sodium (FeNa) < 1%. Responds rapidly to volume restoration.
- Intrinsic Renal AKI: Acute tubular necrosis (ATN, ischemic or nephrotoxic), acute interstitial nephritis (AIN, drug-induced: PPIs, NSAIDs, antibiotics), glomerulonephritis, vascular occlusion. FeNa > 2%, urine sediment contains "muddy brown" granular casts in ATN.
- Postrenal Azotemia: Urinary tract obstruction (benign prostatic hyperplasia, bilateral urolithiasis, pelvic mass, blocked Foley catheter). Renal ultrasound is mandatory to assess for hydronephrosis.

5. NEPHROLITHIASIS (KIDNEY STONES) & COLIC
Types of Renal Calculi:
- Calcium Oxalate (75 - 80%): Most common. Formed in alkaline-to-neutral urine. Avoid excessive dietary oxalates (spinach, rhubarb, almonds, chocolate). Do NOT restrict dietary calcium; normal calcium intake binds oxalate in the gut, reducing urinary excretion.
- Uric Acid (5 - 10%): Radiolucent on plain X-rays, visualized on non-contrast CT. Associated with acidic urine (pH < 5.5), high purine intake, gout, metabolic syndrome. Alkalinization of urine with potassium citrate is curative.
- Struvite (Magnesium Ammonium Phosphate, 10%): "Infection stones" associated with urease-producing bacteria (Proteus mirabilis, Klebsiella). Can form large staghorn calculi requiring surgical nephrolithotomy.
- Cystine (1 - 2%): Genetic cystinuria defect.

Acute Renal Colic Management:
- Analgesia: First-line therapy is IV or oral NSAIDs (Ketorolac, Ibuprofen) which reduce ureteral spasm and intrarenal pressure, superior to opioids for acute colic unless renal failure is present.
- Medical Expulsive Therapy: Alpha-1 adrenergic antagonists (Tamsulosin 0.4 mg once daily) facilitate expulsion of distal ureteral stones 5 - 10 mm in diameter.
- Emergency Red Flags for Stone Intervention: Obstructing stone with concurrent urinary tract infection (urosepsis risk—requires emergent nephrostomy tube or JJ stent), intractable pain/vomiting, bilateral obstruction, or obstruction of a solitary kidney.

6. ELECTROLYTE EMERGENCIES IN NEPHROLOGY
Hyperkalemia (Serum Potassium > 5.0 mEq/L, Critical >= 6.0 mEq/L):
- Etiologies: Decreased renal excretion (CKD/AKI), hypoaldosteronism, potassium-sparing diuretics, ACEi/ARBs, tissue breakdown (rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis).
- ECG Progression: Tall peaked T waves -> PR prolongation -> P wave flattening -> QRS widening -> "sine wave" pattern -> Ventricular fibrillation or asystole.
- Emergent Treatment Protocol:
  1. Membrane Stabilization: IV Calcium Gluconate 10% (10 mL over 2-3 minutes) or Calcium Chloride. Does NOT lower serum potassium; prevents cardiac arrest.
  2. Intracellular Shift: Regular Insulin 10 units IV with 25-50g 50% Dextrose (D50) over 15-30 minutes; nebulized Albuterol (10-20 mg).
  3. Elimination: Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (Lokelma), Patiromer, loop diuretics (Furosemide), or emergent Hemodialysis.

Hyponatremia (Serum Sodium < 135 mEq/L, Critical < 120 mEq/L):
- Assess volume status (hypovolemic, euvolemic, hypervolemic) and urine osmolality.
- Severe symptoms (seizures, coma, herniation): Hypertonic Saline (3% NaCl) 100 mL bolus over 10 minutes, repeated up to 3 times to raise sodium by 4-6 mEq/L.
- Safety Caution: Correction rate MUST NOT exceed 8 mEq/L in 24 hours to prevent Central Pontine Myelinolysis (Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome).

7. LIFESTYLE & NUTRITIONAL PRINCIPLES FOR KIDNEY LONGEVITY
- Dietary Sodium Restriction: < 2,000 mg/day (equivalent to < 5g table salt) to decrease proteinuria and enhance antihypertensive drug efficacy.
- Protein Intake Optimization: In non-dialysis CKD Stages 3-5, target moderate protein intake 0.6 - 0.8 g/kg/day to reduce nitrogenous waste accumulation. Avoid ultra-high protein diets (> 1.5 g/kg/day) which induce glomerular hyperfiltration.
- Nephrotoxic Medication Vigilance: Eliminate unnecessary nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs like Ibuprofen, Naproxen), aminoglycosides, and unverified herbal remedies containing aristolochic acid.
- Hydration Maintenance: Ensure daily fluid intake of 2.5 to 3.0 liters unless fluid-restricted due to advanced CKD Stage 4-5, heart failure, or severe hypoalbuminemia.
