HEALTHBUDDY CLINICAL PROTOCOL QUICK REFERENCE V1
================================================

Purpose
- Provide protocol-style, retrieval-friendly guidance for common high-risk pathways.
- Educational support only; not a replacement for clinical judgment.

Authority tier note
- This file is intended as a higher-authority internal protocol reference.


1) CHEST PAIN TRIAGE (ADULT)
============================

Immediate emergency escalation criteria:
- Chest pressure/pain lasting more than 5 to 10 minutes.
- Pain with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, syncope, or radiation to arm/jaw/back.
- New neurologic deficits or severe weakness with chest symptoms.

Action:
- Call emergency services immediately.
- Do not drive self to hospital.
- If not allergic and no contraindication known, chew standard aspirin while awaiting emergency team.


2) STROKE FAST PROTOCOL
=======================

Screen:
- Face drooping.
- Arm weakness.
- Speech difficulty.
- Time of onset or last known normal.

Action:
- Emergency services now. Time-sensitive stroke pathways depend on rapid presentation.


3) HYPERTENSION URGENCY VS EMERGENCY
====================================

Severe blood pressure elevation with any target-organ symptom (neurologic deficits, chest pain, dyspnea, acute confusion):
- Treat as emergency-level risk and escalate urgently.

Severe elevation without red-flag symptoms:
- Prompt same-day clinician evaluation and medication review.

Self-care support between assessments:
- Sodium reduction, medication adherence, hydration, and stress minimization.


4) DIABETES / HYPERGLYCEMIA RED FLAGS
=====================================

Urgent warning features:
- Persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, dehydration, rapid breathing, confusion, fruity breath, severe drowsiness.

Action:
- Urgent emergency evaluation for possible diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar state.

Routine risk reduction:
- Post-meal walking, high-fiber meal planning, sugary beverage elimination, sleep consistency, regular screening.


5) FEVER & RESPIRATORY ILLNESS TRIAGE
=====================================

Urgent features:
- Breathing difficulty, oxygen concern signs, confusion, dehydration, persistent high fever not improving.

General self-care when no red flags:
- Fluids, rest, fever control as appropriate, monitor progression, and seek care if worsening.


6) SAFE RESPONSE BOUNDARIES FOR CHAT
====================================

The assistant should:
- Distinguish self-care vs urgent vs emergency cues clearly.
- Avoid diagnosis claims.
- Avoid medication dose instructions unless explicitly sourced and clinician-validated.
- Prioritize escalation messaging when red flags are present.


7) QUICK RETRIEVAL PHRASES
==========================

- chest pain emergency protocol
- stroke FAST signs and action
- hypertensive urgency versus emergency
- diabetic ketoacidosis warning signs
- severe dehydration red flags
- self care versus urgent care triage
