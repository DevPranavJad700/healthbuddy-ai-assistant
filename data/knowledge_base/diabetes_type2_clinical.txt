TOPIC: Type 2 Diabetes — Clinical Overview
SOURCE: HealthBuddy Clinical Knowledge Base v3.0
CATEGORY: Endocrinology / Chronic Disease Management

=== DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY ===
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic condition characterised by insulin resistance and progressive beta-cell dysfunction. Unlike Type 1, the pancreas still produces insulin, but cells fail to respond effectively, causing blood glucose to accumulate.

Key driver: visceral adiposity increases free fatty acid flux, promoting hepatic glucose production and impairing peripheral glucose uptake.

=== DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (ADA 2024) ===
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL during 75g OGTT
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (confirmed on repeat)
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss)

Pre-diabetes: FPG 100–125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7–6.4%

=== RISK FACTORS ===
- BMI ≥ 25 (≥23 in Asian populations)
- Physical inactivity
- Family history in first-degree relative
- History of gestational diabetes
- Hypertension (≥140/90 mmHg)
- HDL < 35 mg/dL or triglycerides > 250 mg/dL
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Age ≥ 45 years
- Prior impaired fasting glucose or impaired glucose tolerance
- Ethnicity: South Asian, African American, Hispanic, Native American

=== SYMPTOMS ===
Early: Often asymptomatic. May have fatigue, blurred vision, slow wound healing.
Classic triad: Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia
Advanced: Numbness/tingling in feet (peripheral neuropathy), recurrent infections, erectile dysfunction

=== MANAGEMENT ===
First-line: Metformin + lifestyle intervention (unless contraindicated)
Lifestyle targets:
  - ≥150 min/week moderate aerobic activity
  - Weight loss of 5–10% improves glycaemic control significantly
  - Dietary: Low glycaemic index, Mediterranean or DASH pattern, reduce refined carbohydrates

Medication classes:
- Metformin: reduces hepatic glucose output, weight-neutral, very safe
- SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): renal glucose excretion, cardioprotective, weight loss benefit
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide): powerful HbA1c reduction, weight loss, CV benefit
- DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin): glucose-dependent insulin release, weight-neutral
- Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glyburide): effective but risk of hypoglycaemia and weight gain
- Insulin therapy: required when HbA1c remains above target despite oral agents

HbA1c targets:
- General population: < 7.0%
- Older adults with comorbidities: < 8.0%
- Young, otherwise healthy: < 6.5%

=== MONITORING ===
- HbA1c: every 3 months until stable, then every 6 months
- Annual: lipid panel, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, eye exam (retinopathy), foot exam
- Blood pressure target: < 130/80 mmHg
- eGFR monitoring for kidney disease

=== COMPLICATIONS ===
Microvascular: Diabetic retinopathy (leading cause of adult blindness), nephropathy, peripheral neuropathy
Macrovascular: Coronary artery disease, stroke, peripheral artery disease
Acute: Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS), hypoglycaemia

=== HYPOGLYCAEMIA MANAGEMENT ===
Mild: 15-15 rule — 15g fast-acting carbohydrates (glucose tablets, juice), recheck in 15 min
Severe (unconscious): Glucagon injection or IV dextrose (emergency care)
Threshold for hypoglycaemia: BG < 70 mg/dL

=== FOOT CARE ===
Daily inspection, no walking barefoot, proper footwear, annual podiatry assessment
Any foot ulcer, numbness, or colour change requires urgent medical evaluation
