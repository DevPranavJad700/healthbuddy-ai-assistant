=== 1. OSTEOARTHRITIS VS. RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS: CLINICAL COMPARISON ===
A. Osteoarthritis (OA) — Degenerative Joint Disease:
- Pathophysiology: Progressive mechanical degradation of articular cartilage, subchondral bone remodeling, osteophyte (bone spur) formation, and low-grade secondary synovitis.
- Target Joints: Weight-bearing joints (knees, hips), lumbar and cervical spine, hand distal interphalangeal (DIP — Heberden's nodes) and proximal interphalangeal (PIP — Bouchard's nodes), first carpometacarpal (CMC) base of thumb.
- Pain Pattern: Mechanical pain that worsens with joint use/weight-bearing and improves with rest.
- Morning Stiffness: Brief duration (< 30 minutes), "gelling" phenomenon after periods of inactivity that eases with light movement.
- Physical Findings: Bony joint enlargement, crepitus on passive range of motion, cool joint without marked erythema, restricted flexion/extension.
- Management:
  * First-line Non-pharmacologic: Low-impact aerobic exercise (swimming, cycling, water aerobics), quadriceps and gluteal strengthening, weight loss (every 1 lb lost relieves 4 lbs of compressive pressure across knee joint).
  * First-line Pharmacologic: Topical NSAIDs (Diclofenac gel 1% or 2% TID-QID) preferred over oral NSAIDs due to minimal systemic GI/cardiovascular/renal absorption.
  * Second-line: Oral NSAIDs (Naproxen, Celecoxib) with proton pump inhibitor gastroprotection; intra-articular corticosteroid injections for acute flares (limit to 3-4 per year to prevent cartilage atrophy); total joint arthroplasty for end-stage joint space collapse.

B. Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) — Autoimmune Inflammatory Arthritis:
- Pathophysiology: Autoimmune T-cell and B-cell mediated chronic inflammatory synovitis leading to pannus formation, cartilage erosion, and juxta-articular osteopenia.
- Target Joints: Symmetrical small joint involvement (Metacarpophalangeal / MCP, Proximal interphalangeal / PIP, Metatarsophalangeal / MTP, wrists). DIP joints and thoracolumbar spine are spared.
- Pain Pattern: Inflammatory pain that improves with physical activity and worsens with prolonged rest.
- Morning Stiffness: Prolonged duration (> 60 minutes, frequently lasting hours).
- Systemic Features: Rheumatoid nodules, fatigue, low-grade fever, weight loss, keratoconjunctivitis sicca, interstitial lung disease, pericarditis.
- Laboratory Biomarkers:
  * Rheumatoid Factor (RF): Sensitive (70-80%) but non-specific.
  * Anti-Citrullinated Protein Antibodies (Anti-CCP / ACPA): Highly specific (95-98%) for RA and predicts erosive disease progression.
  * Elevated acute phase reactants: High ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate) and CRP (C-Reactive Protein).
- Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs (DMARDs) — Start within 3 months of diagnosis:
  * Conventional Synthetic DMARD: Methotrexate (oral or subcutaneous weekly; mandatory co-prescription of Folic Acid 1-5 mg daily; monitor CBC, ALT/AST, creatinine every 8-12 weeks; strictly contraindicated in pregnancy).
  * Biologic DMARDs: TNF-alpha inhibitors (Adalimumab, Infliximab, Etanercept), IL-6 receptor antagonists (Tocilizumab), T-cell costimulation blockers (Abatacept), JAK inhibitors (Tofacitinib). Pre-treatment screening for latent tuberculosis (IGRA / Quantiferon) and hepatitis B/C is mandatory.

=== 2. ACUTE LOW BACK PAIN & SCIATICA ===
Epidemiology: Low back pain (LBP) is the single leading cause of years lived with disability worldwide. 85-90% is classified as "non-specific mechanical LBP" lacking serious structural compromise.

Clinical Classification:
1. Acute Non-Specific Mechanical LBP:
   - Muscle strain, ligamentous sprain, facet joint arthrosis.
   - Pain localized to lumbar region, buttocks, or upper posterior thigh. Normal neurological examination (intact reflexes, 5/5 motor strength, preserved sensation).
   - Guideline-Directed Management:
     * Encourage active mobilization: Avoid bed rest (strict bed rest beyond 24-48 hours delays recovery and increases chronic disability).
     * First-line: Heat therapy (heating pad), gentle walking, light stretching.
     * Pharmacologic: First-line NSAIDs (Ibuprofen 400-600 mg TID or Naproxen 500 mg BID with meals) or Acetaminophen. Short course (max 5 days) of non-benzodiazepine skeletal muscle relaxant (Cyclobenzaprine 5-10 mg at night) if severe painful muscle spasm is present.
     * Routine lumbar X-ray or MRI is strongly DISCOURAGED within the first 6 weeks unless red flags are identified.

2. Radiculopathy (Sciatica / Lumbar Disc Herniation):
   - Nerve root compression (most commonly L4-L5 or L5-S1 nerve roots).
   - Sharp, lancinating, shooting pain radiating below the knee in a dermatomal distribution.
   - Physical Examination:
     * Straight Leg Raise (SLR) Test: Pain radiating below knee with passive elevation between 30° and 70° (sensitive for disc herniation).
     * Crossed Straight Leg Raise: Elevation of unaffected leg reproduces sciatica in affected leg (highly specific ~90%).
     * L4 nerve root: Diminished patellar reflex, weakness of ankle dorsiflexion, numbness over medial lower leg.
     * L5 nerve root: Normal reflexes, weakness of great toe extension (extensor hallucis longus), numbness over dorsum of foot.
     * S1 nerve root: Diminished Achilles tendon reflex, weakness of plantar flexion (cannot walk on tiptoes), numbness over lateral foot.

=== 3. CAUDA EQUINA SYNDROME (CRITICAL SPINE EMERGENCY) ===
Compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots of the cauda equina, most commonly from massive central lumbar disc herniation, tumor, epidural abscess, or spinal hematoma.

Cardinal Red Flag Symptoms (Mandates Emergency Department Transfer):
1. Urinary Retention or Overflow Incontinence: Post-void residual volume > 200 mL on bladder ultrasound scan.
2. Saddle Anesthesia: Numbness or loss of sensation in the perineum, buttocks, inner thighs, and perianal region (the area that would touch a horse's saddle).
3. Fecal Incontinence or Loss of Anal Sphincter Tone.
4. Progressive, Bilateral Lower Extremity Motor Weakness (e.g., foot drop, severe leg giving way).

Emergency Protocol:
Immediate stat non-contrast urgent lumbar MRI. Emergency surgical decompressive laminectomy/discectomy within 24–48 hours of onset to prevent permanent paraplegia, neurogenic bladder, and lifelong sexual dysfunction.

=== 4. SOFT TISSUE INJURY PROTOCOL: PEACE & LOVE ===
The outdated "RICE" (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) protocol has been superseded by the evidence-based British Journal of Sports Medicine (BJSM) "PEACE & LOVE" framework, which supports the body's natural inflammatory healing cascade.

Immediate Care (First 1–3 Days) — PEACE:
- P — Protect: Unload or restrict movement for the first 1-3 days to minimize bleeding and tissue disruption.
- E — Elevate: Elevate the injured limb higher than the heart to promote interstitial fluid drainage.
- A — Avoid Anti-inflammatory Modalities: Avoid NSAIDs and ice baths in acute phase. Acute inflammation is a biological necessity for tissue repair and collagen synthesis; blunting it impairs long-term mechanical tissue strength.
- C — Compress: Elastic bandage or taping to limit intra-articular effusion and tissue edema.
- E — Educate: Teach patient about active recovery; avoid passive modalities and excessive medicalization.

Subsequent Rehabilitation (After Day 3) — LOVE:
- L — Load: Gradual mechanical loading without pain stimulates cellular repair and remodels tissue fibers (mechanotransduction).
- O — Optimism: Brain and mindset influence pain perception; condition patient for positive outcome.
- V — Vascularization: Pain-free cardiovascular aerobic exercise increases blood flow to repairing tissues.
- E — Exercise: Target mobility, proprioception, and progressive strength exercises to restore full functional capacity and prevent reinjury.

=== 5. CARPAL TUNNEL SYNDROME & WORKPLACE ERGONOMICS ===
Pathophysiology: Compression of the median nerve within the fibro-osseous carpal tunnel at the wrist.
Symptoms: Paresthesias, numbness, and burning pain affecting the thumb, index finger, middle finger, and radial half of the ring finger. Classically awakens patient from sleep; shaken hands provide temporary relief ("flick sign"). Thenar muscle atrophy and weakness of thumb abduction in severe chronic cases.
Clinical Tests: Phalen's maneuver (sustained 60-second forced wrist flexion reproduces numbness) and Tinel's sign (percussion over volar carpal tunnel produces electric sensations).
Conservative Management: Neutral wrist splint worn during sleep for 6-8 weeks; ergonomic workstation adjustments (forearms parallel to floor, keyboard at elbow height, neutral wrist posture); local corticosteroid injection; surgical carpal tunnel release for refractory symptoms or thenar muscle wasting.
