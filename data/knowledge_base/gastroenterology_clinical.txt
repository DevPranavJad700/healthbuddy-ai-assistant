=== GUT MICROBIOME ===
The human gut contains ~100 trillion microorganisms (microbiome); 1.5–2kg by weight
Functions: Digestion of complex carbohydrates, vitamin synthesis (K, B12, folate), immune modulation, gut-brain axis signalling
Dysbiosis: Imbalance linked to IBD, IBS, obesity, depression, autoimmune disease

Promoting microbiome diversity:
- Eat 30+ different plant foods/week (vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds)
- Fermented foods: yoghurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut, miso — add beneficial bacteria
- Prebiotic fibre (food for bacteria): garlic, onion, leeks, asparagus, oats, bananas
- Avoid unnecessary antibiotics; they deplete microbiome diversity for up to 12 months
- Probiotic supplements: evidence strongest for: antibiotic-associated diarrhoea, C. diff prevention, IBS, acute infectious diarrhoea

=== IRRITABLE BOWEL SYNDROME (IBS) ===
Functional GI disorder — altered bowel habits + abdominal pain, no structural pathology
Affects 10–15% of population; twice as common in women; often associated with anxiety/depression

Rome IV criteria: Recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week for ≥3 months, associated with ≥2 of:
1. Related to defecation
2. Change in stool frequency
3. Change in stool form (appearance)

Subtypes:
- IBS-D (diarrhoea predominant): loose/watery stools, urgency, frequency
- IBS-C (constipation predominant): hard/lumpy stools, straining, incomplete evacuation
- IBS-M (mixed): alternates between D and C

Triggers: Stress, specific foods (high-FODMAP), hormonal changes, gut infections (post-infectious IBS)

Management:
- Low-FODMAP diet: Elimination then reintroduction phase; reduces symptoms in 70% (requires dietitian guidance)
  FODMAPs = Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides And Polyols (lactose, fructose, wheat, onion, garlic, legumes)
- Fibre: Soluble fibre (psyllium) helps both IBS-C and IBS-D; insoluble fibre may worsen IBS-D
- Medications: Antispasmodics (buscopan) for pain; loperamide for IBS-D; laxatives for IBS-C; low-dose TCAs or SSRIs for gut-brain targeting
- Gut-directed hypnotherapy: 70% response rate in trials
- Stress management and CBT: Significant benefit

=== ACID REFLUX & GORD/GERD ===
Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease: Stomach acid backs up into oesophagus repeatedly

Symptoms:
- Heartburn: Burning sensation in chest, worse after meals, lying down, bending
- Regurgitation: Acid taste in mouth
- Non-cardiac chest pain, chronic cough (nocturnal), hoarse voice, laryngitis, dental erosion

Alarm symptoms (require urgent endoscopy): Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), unintentional weight loss, GI bleeding, persistent vomiting, iron-deficiency anaemia

Lifestyle modifications:
- Elevate head of bed 15–20cm (wedge pillow)
- Avoid large meals, eat ≥3h before lying down
- Avoid: coffee, alcohol, chocolate, fatty foods, citrus, mint (relax LOS)
- Weight loss: BMI reduction of 3.5 improves GERD significantly
- Stop smoking (reduces LOS pressure)

Treatment:
- Antacids (calcium carbonate, Gaviscon): Rapid but short-lived relief
- H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine): Reduces acid production; good for mild-moderate GERD
- Proton Pump Inhibitors (omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole): Most effective; take 30 min before breakfast for best effect; use lowest effective dose; do not use indefinitely without review
- PPI risks with long-term use: B12 deficiency, hypomagnesaemia, C. diff risk, possible fracture risk (minimal)

Complications: Oesophagitis, Barrett's oesophagus (precancerous metaplasia) → regular endoscopic surveillance

=== PEPTIC ULCER DISEASE ===
Ulcers in gastric or duodenal mucosa
Causes: H. pylori infection (80% of duodenal ulcers, 60% of gastric ulcers), NSAID use

Symptoms: Epigastric pain; duodenal ulcer pain relieved by food; gastric ulcer pain may worsen with eating

H. pylori eradication (triple therapy 7–14 days):
- PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin (or metronidazole)
- Confirm eradication with urea breath test 4 weeks after completing antibiotics

Alarm features requiring urgent endoscopy: Haematemesis (vomiting blood), melaena (black tarry stool), severe sudden-onset epigastric pain, syncope

=== INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE (IBD) ===

Crohn's Disease:
- Affects any part of GI tract (mouth to anus); transmural inflammation; skip lesions
- Symptoms: Diarrhoea, abdominal pain (often right lower quadrant), weight loss, fatigue, fever
- Perianal disease: fistulae, abscesses (pathognomonic for Crohn's)
- Extraintestinal: Arthritis, uveitis, erythema nodosum, primary sclerosing cholangitis

Ulcerative Colitis:
- Confined to colon; continuous mucosal inflammation from rectum proximally
- Symptoms: Bloody diarrhoea, mucus, urgency, tenesmus (feeling of incomplete evacuation)
- Risk of colorectal cancer with long-standing pan-colitis: colonoscopy surveillance essential

IBD Treatment:
- Induction: Corticosteroids (prednisolone) for flares — not for maintenance
- Maintenance: Aminosalicylates (5-ASA) for UC; immunomodulators (azathioprine, mercaptopurine) for Crohn's
- Biologics: Anti-TNF (infliximab, adalimumab), anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab)
- Surgery: 20–30% of Crohn's patients, 10–30% of UC patients require surgery

=== CONSTIPATION ===
Infrequent bowel movements (<3/week), hard/difficult stools, straining, incomplete evacuation
Chronic constipation: affects 15% of population

Causes: Inadequate fibre/fluid, physical inactivity, medications (opioids, iron, antacids, anticholinergics), hypothyroidism, bowel dysmotility

Management (stepwise):
1. Lifestyle: Increase fibre (25–35g/day), 2L water/day, regular exercise, do not ignore defecation urge, consistent morning routine (gastrocolic reflex)
2. Bulk-forming laxatives (psyllium, methylcellulose): safest; take with plenty of water
3. Osmotic laxatives (macrogol/polyethylene glycol, lactulose): draw water into bowel; first-line for chronic constipation
4. Stimulant laxatives (senna, bisacodyl): for acute relief; not for daily long-term use
5. Stool softeners (docusate): modest effect; useful when straining harmful (post-surgery, cardiac)
6. Enemas/suppositories: For impaction or acute severe constipation

Red flags requiring colonoscopy: New onset constipation >50 years, rectal bleeding, weight loss, family history of CRC, iron-deficiency anaemia

=== DIARRHOEA ===

Acute (<2 weeks): Usually infectious (viral, bacterial, parasitic)
- Gastroenteritis: Norovirus most common; self-limiting 24–72h; MAIN RISK = dehydration
- Oral rehydration solution (ORS): 1L water + 6 tsp sugar + 0.5 tsp salt, or WHO ORS sachets
- Antibiotics for: bloody diarrhoea (suspected Shigella, Campylobacter, E. coli O157 — but NOT metronidazole for E. coli O157), confirmed Clostridioides difficile (oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin), traveller's diarrhoea (azithromycin)
- AVOID: antidiarrhoeal medications if bloody stool or high fever (may worsen bacterial colitis)

Chronic (>4 weeks): Warrants investigation — IBD, IBS, malabsorption (coeliac, lactose intolerance), microscopic colitis, medications

=== COELIAC DISEASE ===
Autoimmune response to gluten (wheat, barley, rye) damaging small intestinal villi

Symptoms: Variable — diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, bloating, weight loss, anaemia, mouth ulcers, dermatitis herpetiformis, osteoporosis, infertility
Silent coeliac: Common (1% of population); diagnosed incidentally

Diagnosis: Anti-TTG IgA antibody (first-line); confirmed by duodenal biopsy (Marsh classification)
Treatment: Strict lifelong gluten-free diet; even small amounts cause damage
Monitor: Annual review, DEXA scan, check for other autoimmune conditions (thyroid, T1DM)
