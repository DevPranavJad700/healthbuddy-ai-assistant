=== PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES (WHO 2020) ===

Adults (18–64 years):
- 150–300 min/week moderate-intensity aerobic activity, OR
- 75–150 min/week vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, OR
- Equivalent combination
- Muscle-strengthening activities: ≥2 days/week (all major muscle groups)

Older adults (≥65 years):
- Same aerobic recommendations as adults
- Add: Balance and coordination exercises ≥3 days/week to prevent falls
- Multicomponent exercise (aerobic + strength + balance) most beneficial

Children (5–17 years):
- ≥60 min/day moderate-to-vigorous intensity physical activity
- Vigorous and muscle-strengthening: ≥3 days/week

Benefits of meeting guidelines:
- All-cause mortality ↓30%
- CVD mortality ↓35%
- Hypertension incidence ↓27%
- T2DM incidence ↓25%
- Breast and colon cancer risk ↓20%
- Depression and anxiety ↓30%
- Cognitive decline ↓35%
- Fall-related injuries in elderly ↓30%

=== TYPES OF EXERCISE ===

Aerobic (Cardiovascular) Exercise:
- Definition: Rhythmic, sustained activities using large muscle groups; increases heart rate
- Examples: walking, running, cycling, swimming, dancing, rowing
- Moderate intensity: 50–70% max HR; able to speak in sentences (brisk walk)
- Vigorous intensity: 70–85% max HR; can only speak a few words (jogging, cycling fast)
- Maximum heart rate (estimated): 220 – age
- Benefits: Improved cardiac output, reduced BP, better insulin sensitivity, weight management, mental health

Resistance/Strength Training:
- Stimulates muscle hypertrophy and strength via progressive overload
- Recommendations: 2–3 sets, 8–12 repetitions, 60–80% 1-rep max; 48h rest between same muscle groups
- Benefits: Preserves muscle mass (critical after age 30: lose 3–8% per decade without training), increases metabolic rate, improves insulin sensitivity, bone density, joint stability
- Older adults: resistance training most critical intervention to prevent sarcopenia

High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT):
- Alternating max-effort bursts (20–40 sec) with brief recovery
- Superior time efficiency; similar or better outcomes than MICT for cardiovascular health
- Not suitable for beginners or those with joint problems; requires proper warm-up
- Caution: Stop if chest pain, dizziness, severe shortness of breath

Flexibility & Mobility:
- Static stretching: Hold 15–60 sec; best after workout when muscles are warm
- Dynamic stretching: Controlled movements through range of motion; best as warm-up
- Yoga/Pilates: Combine flexibility, strength, balance; evidence for low back pain, stress, BP

Balance Training:
- Critical for fall prevention in elderly
- Examples: Single-leg stand, heel-toe walking, Tai Chi
- Tai Chi: RCT evidence for fall prevention (40% reduction in frequent fallers)

=== EXERCISE PRESCRIPTION BY CONDITION ===

Hypertension:
- Aerobic exercise is antihypertensive: reduces SBP by 4–9 mmHg
- Resistance training: additional 2–3 mmHg reduction
- Isometric exercise (wall sits, plank): emerging evidence for BP reduction

Type 2 Diabetes:
- Aerobic + resistance training combined most effective for HbA1c reduction
- Post-meal walks (10 min) significantly blunt postprandial glucose spikes
- HbA1c reduction: ~0.7% with structured exercise programme

Heart Disease (Stable):
- Cardiac rehabilitation: supervised exercise programme post-MI or revascularisation
- Start low, progress gradually; ECG monitoring initially
- Absolute contraindications to exercise: unstable angina, decompensated HF, severe aortic stenosis

Osteoporosis:
- Weight-bearing aerobic exercise (walking, dancing, not swimming/cycling) stimulates bone formation
- Resistance training: essential for bone density maintenance
- Avoid high-impact exercise in severe osteoporosis (fracture risk)

Depression:
- Exercise as effective as antidepressants for mild-moderate depression (Blumenthal JAMA 1999, Kvam 2016)
- 30 min moderate aerobic exercise 3x/week demonstrably reduces depressive symptoms
- Mechanism: Endorphins, BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), normalises HPA axis

Chronic Low Back Pain:
- Exercise IS the treatment (not rest) — bed rest worsens outcomes
- Core stabilisation, walking, swimming most beneficial
- Yoga and Pilates: RCT evidence for pain reduction

=== SPORTS INJURIES — COMMON PRESENTATIONS ===

RICE Protocol (acute soft tissue injuries):
- Rest: Avoid activities that worsen pain (24–48h, not prolonged)
- Ice: 20 min every 2–3h for first 48h; wrap in cloth (never apply direct to skin)
- Compression: Elastic bandage to reduce swelling
- Elevation: Raise injured limb above heart level
(PEACE & LOVE framework now preferred: Protection, Elevation, Avoid anti-inflammatories, Compression, Education; Load, Optimism, Vascularisation, Exercise)

Ankle sprains (most common sports injury):
- Lateral (inversion): most common; involves ATFL
- Grade I (stretch), II (partial tear), III (complete tear)
- Treatment: Early mobilisation, physiotherapy; surgery rarely needed even for Grade III

Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) Injury:
- Non-contact mechanism (sudden change of direction, landing)
- "Pop" heard/felt, immediate swelling, instability
- MRI confirms diagnosis; surgical reconstruction for active individuals

Stress fractures:
- Overuse injury; common in runners (tibia, metatarsals)
- Gradual onset, localised bone pain, worse with activity
- Bone scan/MRI most sensitive; rest 6–8 weeks, then graduated return

Runner's Knee (Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome):
- Anterior knee pain aggravated by stairs, squatting, prolonged sitting
- Treatment: Quadriceps strengthening, hip abductor strengthening, activity modification

=== EXERCISE AND HYDRATION ===
- Thirst = already mildly dehydrated; drink proactively during exercise >60 min
- 500mL water 2h before exercise; 150–250mL every 15–20 min during
- Sports drinks (electrolytes): beneficial for exercise >60–90 min; not needed for shorter sessions
- Heat illness: heat cramps → heat exhaustion → heat stroke (medical emergency)
  - Heat stroke: core temp >40°C, confusion, no sweating → call emergency services, cool rapidly

=== SAFE RETURN TO SPORT AFTER ILLNESS ===
Myocarditis (inflammation of heart muscle): Can follow viral illness (including COVID-19)
- Complete cardiac rest until cleared by cardiologist
- Premature return = risk of fatal arrhythmia
- Symptoms to watch: chest pain, palpitations, excessive dyspnoea after mild exercise → urgent evaluation

General illness: Rest during fever; gradual return (50% effort day 1, 75% day 2, full day 3 if no symptoms)
