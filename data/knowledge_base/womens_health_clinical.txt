=== MENSTRUAL HEALTH ===

Normal menstrual cycle:
- Cycle length: 21–35 days (average 28)
- Menstruation duration: 2–7 days
- Flow: 20–80 mL total; 6–8 pads/tampons per day = normal max
- Menarche: typically 11–15 years
- Menopause: average age 51 (range 45–55)

Abnormal uterine bleeding definitions:
- Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (HMB): >80 mL/cycle, soaking pad in <1 hour, passage of clots >2.5cm
- Dysmenorrhoea: Painful periods. Primary (no structural cause) — prostaglandin-mediated. Secondary — endometriosis, fibroids, adenomyosis
- Amenorrhoea: Absent periods. Primary (never started by age 15) or secondary (absent for ≥3 months in previously regular cycles)
- Oligomenorrhoea: Infrequent cycles (>35 days between periods)
- Intermenstrual bleeding: Bleeding between periods — always warrants evaluation

Management of dysmenorrhoea:
- First-line: NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400mg TDS starting day before period), heat therapy
- Second-line: Hormonal contraception (suppresses prostaglandins)
- Refractory: Consider laparoscopy to rule out endometriosis

=== POLYCYSTIC OVARY SYNDROME (PCOS) ===
Most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age (6–12% prevalence)

Rotterdam criteria (2/3 required):
1. Oligo/anovulation (irregular or absent periods)
2. Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism (acne, hirsutism, elevated testosterone)
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (≥12 follicles, ovarian volume >10mL)

Associated features:
- Insulin resistance (70–80% of women with PCOS)
- Obesity (50–80%)
- Increased risk of T2DM, endometrial cancer, cardiovascular disease
- Infertility (anovulation is the most common cause of infertility in PCOS)

Management:
- Lifestyle: Weight loss of 5–10% can restore ovulation and improve all features
- Irregular periods/contraception: Combined oral contraceptive pill (COCP)
- Hirsutism: Anti-androgens (spironolactone), laser hair removal
- Fertility: Clomiphene or letrozole for ovulation induction; metformin adjunct
- Metabolic: Metformin improves insulin sensitivity
- Mental health: Higher rates of anxiety and depression in PCOS — screen and treat

=== ENDOMETRIOSIS ===
Endometrial tissue grows outside the uterus → inflammation, adhesions, pain

Symptoms:
- Severe dysmenorrhoea (often out of proportion to period)
- Deep dyspareunia (pain during/after sex)
- Chronic pelvic pain
- Painful bowel/bladder symptoms (especially during period)
- Subfertility

Average diagnosis delay: 7–10 years (often dismissed as "normal period pain")

Management:
- Medical: NSAIDs, hormonal suppression (COCP, progesterone, GnRH agonists)
- Surgical: Laparoscopic excision/ablation for severe cases
- Fertility treatment when needed (IVF often effective)

=== CONTRACEPTION ===

Hormonal methods:
- Combined oral contraceptive pill (COCP): 99% effective with perfect use; reduces dysmenorrhoea and HMB; avoid in migraine with aura, smoking >35yrs, DVT history
- Progestogen-only pill (mini-pill): Safe in breastfeeding, hypertension; must take at same time daily
- Implant (Nexplanon): 99.9% effective, 3-year duration; most reliable reversible method
- Injectable (Depo-Provera): 3-monthly; may delay return of fertility by 12–18 months
- IUS (Mirena/Kyleena): 99.9% effective, 3–8 years; reduces menstrual flow significantly

Non-hormonal methods:
- Copper IUD: 99.9% effective, 5–10 years; may worsen menorrhagia initially; emergency contraception within 5 days of unprotected sex
- Condoms (male): 98% perfect use, 85% typical use; only method preventing STIs

Emergency contraception:
- Levonorgestrel (Plan B): Within 72 hours (up to 120h); less effective >72h, in overweight women
- Ulipristal acetate (EllaOne): Within 120 hours; more effective than levonorgestrel
- Copper IUD: Most effective EC (>99%), can be left in for ongoing contraception

=== PREGNANCY BASICS ===

Antenatal care schedule:
- First visit: 8–10 weeks — confirm pregnancy, dating scan, blood tests (blood group, rubella, STIs, FBC)
- 12 weeks: Nuchal translucency scan + combined first trimester screening (trisomies)
- 20 weeks: Anomaly scan (detailed fetal anatomy)
- 28 weeks: GDM screening (OGTT), anti-D if Rh-negative
- 36–40 weeks: Group B Strep swab, birth planning

Key supplements in pregnancy:
- Folic acid 400–800 mcg/day: from preconception to 12 weeks (400mcg; 5mg if high risk)
- Vitamin D 1,000–2,000 IU/day
- Iron only if deficient (test at first visit)
- Iodine 150mcg/day

Dangerous in pregnancy (avoid):
- Raw fish, unpasteurised cheese, deli meats (Listeria risk)
- Vitamin A supplements (>3,000 IU/day: teratogenic)
- Alcohol (no safe amount established)
- Caffeine (limit to <200mg/day)
- NSAIDs (especially third trimester)
- ACE inhibitors, ARBs, statins, methotrexate, warfarin (first trimester)

Common pregnancy symptoms:
- Nausea: peaks 6–12 weeks; ginger, small frequent meals, vitamin B6 help; severe vomiting (hyperemesis gravidarum) needs IV fluids
- Round ligament pain: stretching of uterine ligaments — normal
- Heartburn: common in third trimester (antacids safe; PPI if severe)
- Pelvic girdle pain: physiotherapy, supportive belt

When to seek urgent care in pregnancy:
- Vaginal bleeding at any stage
- Severe abdominal pain
- Decreased fetal movement after 24 weeks
- Severe headache, visual changes, oedema (pre-eclampsia signs)
- Signs of DVT (leg pain, redness, swelling)
- Rupture of membranes (fluid leaking)
- Fever >38°C

=== MENOPAUSE ===
Menopause = 12 consecutive months without menstruation
Perimenopause: 4–10 years before menopause; irregular cycles, symptoms begin

Symptoms:
- Vasomotor: Hot flushes (most common), night sweats
- Genitourinary: Vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, urinary urgency, recurrent UTIs
- Sleep: Insomnia
- Mood: Irritability, low mood, anxiety
- Cognitive: Brain fog, memory difficulties
- Long-term: Accelerated bone loss (osteoporosis risk), cardiovascular risk increase

Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):
- Most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms
- Types: Combined (oestrogen + progestogen for women with uterus), oestrogen-only (post-hysterectomy)
- Benefits vs risks: Reduces fractures, symptoms; small increase in breast cancer risk with combined HRT (roughly 1 extra case per 200 women over 10 years)
- Absolute contraindications: oestrogen-sensitive cancer, unexplained vaginal bleeding, active DVT/PE
- Local oestrogen (vaginal cream/pessary): effective for genitourinary symptoms; minimal systemic absorption

Non-hormonal options:
- SSRIs/SNRIs: Reduce hot flushes ~50%
- Gabapentin: Effective for hot flushes
- Cognitive behavioural therapy: Effective for sleep and mood
- Lifestyle: Layered clothing, cool environment, reduce triggers (alcohol, caffeine, spicy food)
