=== 1. THYROID DISORDERS OVERVIEW ===
The thyroid gland regulates basal metabolic rate, thermogenesis, cardiac chronotropy, and protein synthesis via thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3).
Diagnostic Reference Ranges (Adults):
- Serum TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone): 0.45 – 4.5 mIU/L (Primary screening marker)
- Free T4 (Thyroxine): 0.8 – 1.8 ng/dL (10 – 23 pmol/L)
- Free T3 (Triiodothyronine): 2.3 – 4.2 pg/mL (3.5 – 6.5 pmol/L)
- Anti-TPO Antibodies: < 35 IU/mL (positive in autoimmune Hashimoto's)
- Anti-Tg (Thyroglobulin) Antibodies: < 20 IU/mL

=== 2. HYPOTHYROIDISM (UNDERACTIVE THYROID) ===
Etiology:
- Hashimoto's Thyroiditis (autoimmune lymphocytic destruction of follicles; most common in iodine-sufficient regions)
- Post-ablative (radioactive iodine I-131, thyroidectomy)
- Drug-induced: Amiodarone, Lithium, Checkpoint inhibitors (immunotherapy)
- Iodine deficiency (globally) or congenital dyshormonogenesis

Clinical Presentation:
- Constitutional: Unexplained weight gain, profound fatigue, cold intolerance, hypothermia
- Neurological & Mental: Brain fog, memory sluggishness, depression, slow relaxation phase of deep tendon reflexes (Woltman's sign)
- Dermatological: Coarse dry skin, brittle nails, diffuse alopecia, non-pitting myxedema (periorbital and pretibial)
- Cardiovascular: Bradycardia (< 60 bpm), diastolic hypertension, pericardial effusion
- Gastrointestinal: Chronic constipation, gastric hypomotility
- Reproductive: Menorrhagia, oligomenorrhea, subfertility

Medical Management:
- Levothyroxine (synthetic T4) is the gold-standard monotherapy.
- Initial replacement dose: 1.6 mcg/kg/day ideal body weight in young healthy adults.
- Elderly or coronary artery disease patients: Start low (25–50 mcg/day) to prevent cardiac arrhythmias or angina exacerbation.
- Crucial Administration Guidelines:
  * Take on an empty stomach with a full glass of water, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, or at bedtime (at least 3–4 hours after dinner).
  * Avoid co-administration: Calcium carbonate, ferrous sulfate (iron), aluminum antacids, and soy supplements bind levothyroxine and must be spaced at least 4 hours apart.
- Monitoring: Retest serum TSH 6–8 weeks after any dose adjustment. Once normalized, monitor annually.

=== 3. HYPERTHYROIDISM & THYROTOXICOSIS ===
Etiology:
- Graves' Disease (TSH receptor-stimulating autoantibodies / TRAb; 70-80% of cases)
- Toxic Multinodular Goiter (Plummer's disease; autonomous nodules)
- Toxic Adenoma (single hyperfunctioning nodule)
- Subacute (de Quervain's) Thyroiditis (painful post-viral destructive release of preformed hormone)

Clinical Presentation:
- Tachycardia (resting HR > 100 bpm), palpitations, atrial fibrillation in the elderly
- Heat intolerance, diaphoresis, warm moist skin, fine resting hand tremor
- Unintentional weight loss despite increased appetite (hyperphagia)
- Hyperdefecation, frequent loose stools
- Anxiety, emotional lability, insomnia, fine hair thinning
- Graves' Specific Features: Exophthalmos/proptosis, lid lag, pretibial myxedema (peau d'orange dermopathy)

Management:
- Symptom Control: Beta-blockers (Propranolol 20-40 mg TID or Atenolol 25-50 mg daily) block adrenergic overdrive and reduce peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion.
- Antithyroid Drugs (Thionamides):
  * Methimazole (preferred due to lower hepatotoxicity and once-daily dosing; starting 10-30 mg daily).
  * Propylthiouracil (PTU; preferred in first trimester of pregnancy and thyroid storm due to peripheral T4 conversion block).
  * Warning: Monitor for agranulocytosis (sore throat, fever) requiring immediate CBC with differential.
- Definitive: Radioactive Iodine (I-131) ablation or total thyroidectomy.

=== 4. ADRENAL DISORDERS & CORTISOL REGULATION ===
Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease):
- Primary: Autoimmune destruction of adrenal cortex; deficiency of cortisol, aldosterone, and androgens.
- Secondary: Pituitary ACTH deficiency or abrupt cessation of chronic exogenous corticosteroid therapy.
- Symptoms: Hyperpigmentation (palmar creases, buccal mucosa in primary), orthostatic hypotension, salt craving, hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia.
- Maintenance: Oral Hydrocortisone (15-25 mg daily divided TID) + Fludrocortisone (0.05-0.1 mg daily).
- "Sick-Day Rules": Double or triple the hydrocortisone dose during acute illness, trauma, or high fever to prevent crisis.

Cushing's Syndrome (Hypercortisolemia):
- Etiology: Exogenous steroid use (most common), Pituitary ACTH adenoma (Cushing's disease), Ectopic ACTH (small cell lung carcinoma), Adrenal adenoma.
- Features: Central adiposity, "moon facies", "buffalo hump" (dorsocervical fat pad), violaceous abdominal striae (> 1 cm wide), proximal muscle weakness, osteoporosis, fragile skin with easy bruising, hypertension, glucose intolerance.

=== 5. METABOLIC SYNDROME & HORMONAL HEALTH (PCOS) ===
Metabolic Syndrome Diagnostic Criteria (NCEP ATP III - Any 3 of 5):
1. Waist circumference: > 102 cm (men), > 88 cm (women)
2. Triglycerides: ≥ 150 mg/dL (1.7 mmol/L)
3. HDL Cholesterol: < 40 mg/dL (men), < 50 mg/dL (women)
4. Blood Pressure: ≥ 130/85 mmHg or receiving antihypertensives
5. Fasting Blood Glucose: ≥ 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) or receiving therapy

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (Rotterdam Criteria - 2 of 3):
1. Oligo- or anovulation (irregular or absent menses)
2. Hyperandrogenism (clinical hirsutism, cystic acne, androgenic alopecia, or elevated free testosterone)
3. Polycystic ovaries on pelvic ultrasound (≥ 12 follicles per ovary 2-9 mm, or ovarian volume > 10 mL)
- Management: Weight loss (5-10% body weight restores ovulatory cycles), Metformin for insulin resistance, combined oral contraceptives for cycle regulation and hirsutism.

=== 6. CRITICAL RED FLAGS & EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS ===
1. Myxedema Coma (Severe Hypothyroid Emergency):
   - Triad: Hypothermia (< 35°C / 95°F), altered mental status/coma, bradycardia/hypotension.
   - Action: Dial 911 immediately. Requires intensive care, IV hydrocortisone followed by IV levothyroxine, passive rewarming.
2. Thyroid Storm (Life-Threatening Hyperthyroidism):
   - Presentation: Hyperpyrexia (> 40°C / 104°F), marked tachycardia (> 140 bpm), delirium/psychosis, high-output heart failure, jaundice, nausea/vomiting.
   - Action: Emergency resuscitation, IV beta-blockade, PTU/methimazole, potassium iodide, IV corticosteroids.
3. Acute Adrenal Crisis:
   - Presentation: Severe refractory hypotension/shock, intractable vomiting, abdominal pain, severe lethargy, hypoglycemia.
   - Action: Immediate IM or IV Hydrocortisone (100 mg stat) + 0.9% Normal Saline fluid resuscitation.
