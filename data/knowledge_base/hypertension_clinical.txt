TOPIC: Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) — Clinical Overview
SOURCE: HealthBuddy Clinical Knowledge Base v3.0
CATEGORY: Cardiology / Chronic Disease Management

=== DEFINITION ===
Hypertension is sustained elevation of blood pressure above 130/80 mmHg (ACC/AHA 2017 guidelines). It is the most common modifiable cardiovascular risk factor, affecting ~1.3 billion adults globally.

Classification:
- Normal: < 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120–129 / < 80 mmHg
- Stage 1 HTN: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 HTN: ≥ 140 / ≥ 90 mmHg
- Hypertensive crisis: > 180 / > 120 mmHg (seek emergency care)

=== SYMPTOMS ===
Known as the "silent killer" — most patients have NO symptoms.
Symptoms when present: headache (especially occipital), blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, nosebleeds
Hypertensive emergency: severe headache, visual changes, confusion, chest pain → call emergency services

=== CAUSES ===
Primary (Essential) Hypertension (95%):
- No single identified cause
- Driven by: genetic factors, high sodium intake, obesity, physical inactivity, chronic stress, alcohol, ageing

Secondary Hypertension (5%):
- Renal artery stenosis
- Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn's syndrome)
- Obstructive sleep apnoea
- Chronic kidney disease
- Pheochromocytoma
- Thyroid disorders

=== MEASUREMENT TECHNIQUE ===
- Sit quietly for 5 minutes before measurement
- Two readings, 1–2 min apart, average them
- Use validated cuff, properly sized (small cuff → falsely high reading)
- Confirm hypertension on at least 2 separate occasions
- Home BP monitoring: target < 135/85 mmHg

=== LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS (FIRST-LINE) ===
DASH Diet: rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy; reduces BP by 8–14 mmHg
Sodium restriction: < 2,300 mg/day (ideally < 1,500 mg); reduces BP by 2–8 mmHg
Weight loss: 1 mmHg per kg of weight lost
Exercise: 150 min/week moderate aerobic activity; reduces BP by 4–9 mmHg
Limit alcohol: ≤ 2 drinks/day (men), ≤ 1 drink/day (women)
Quit smoking: does not lower BP directly but dramatically reduces cardiovascular risk
Stress management: mindfulness, sleep hygiene

=== PHARMACOTHERAPY ===
First-line agents (any can be used; choice guided by comorbidities):

ACE Inhibitors (e.g., lisinopril, enalapril):
- Preferred in: diabetes with proteinuria, CKD, heart failure, post-MI
- Side effect: dry persistent cough (switch to ARB if bothersome)
- Avoid in pregnancy (teratogenic)

ARBs (e.g., losartan, valsartan):
- Same indications as ACE inhibitors; no cough
- Avoid in pregnancy

Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., amlodipine, diltiazem):
- Preferred in: elderly, isolated systolic HTN, Black patients
- Side effects: peripheral oedema, flushing

Thiazide Diuretics (e.g., hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone):
- Preferred in: elderly, isolated systolic HTN, edema
- Side effects: hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia

Beta-Blockers (e.g., metoprolol, carvedilol):
- Preferred when: heart failure, angina, post-MI, rate control
- Avoid as first-line without compelling indication

=== TARGET BP GOALS ===
- General adults: < 130/80 mmHg
- Elderly (≥65 years, non-institutionalised): < 130/80 mmHg
- Diabetes: < 130/80 mmHg
- CKD: < 130/80 mmHg (with proteinuria: < 125/75 mmHg)
- Pregnancy: < 140/90 mmHg (avoid ACE/ARBs)

=== MONITORING ===
- Home BP log: morning + evening readings, 7 consecutive days before each appointment
- Routine labs: BMP (electrolytes, creatinine), urinalysis, lipid panel, ECG
- Check for end-organ damage: fundoscopy (retinopathy), LVH on ECG/echo, microalbuminuria

=== COMPLICATIONS OF UNCONTROLLED HTN ===
- Heart attack and heart failure
- Stroke (haemorrhagic and ischaemic)
- Chronic kidney disease
- Hypertensive retinopathy → vision loss
- Peripheral artery disease
- Aortic aneurysm / dissection
