Section 1: Blood Pressure Basics
- Normal adult resting blood pressure is generally around 120/80 mmHg.
- Stage 1 hypertension is commonly defined as systolic 130-139 mmHg or diastolic 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 hypertension is commonly defined as systolic at least 140 mmHg or diastolic at least 90 mmHg.
- A hypertensive crisis often refers to blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg and requires urgent medical evaluation.

Section 2: Diabetes Markers
- A fasting plasma glucose value of 126 mg/dL or higher on more than one test supports diabetes diagnosis.
- A fasting plasma glucose value between 100 and 125 mg/dL is typically considered prediabetes.
- An HbA1c value of 6.5% or higher supports diabetes diagnosis.
- Typical HbA1c target for many non-pregnant adults with diabetes is around 7%, but goals must be individualized by clinicians.

Section 3: Oral Rehydration and Diarrhea
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) helps replace fluids and electrolytes lost during diarrhea.
- For mild dehydration, frequent small sips are usually better tolerated than large volumes at once.
- Warning signs that need urgent care include persistent vomiting, blood in stool, confusion, and reduced urination.

Section 4: Respiratory Infections and Antibiotics
- Most common colds are viral and do not improve with antibiotics.
- Unnecessary antibiotic use increases side effects and antimicrobial resistance.
- For common viral respiratory illness, supportive care often includes hydration, rest, and fever control when needed.

Section 5: Sleep Hygiene
- Adults generally need about 7 to 9 hours of sleep per night.
- Consistent sleep and wake times support better sleep quality.
- Caffeine late in the day can delay sleep onset in sensitive individuals.

Section 6: Safety Notes
- This document is educational and not a substitute for diagnosis or treatment.
- Any chest pain with shortness of breath should be treated as an emergency and evaluated immediately.
