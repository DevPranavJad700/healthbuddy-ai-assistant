=== CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (CAD) ===

Pathophysiology: Atherosclerotic plaque builds in coronary arteries → narrows lumen → reduces oxygen delivery to myocardium.
Stable angina: Chest pain/pressure on exertion, relieved by rest or nitroglycerin
Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS): Plaque rupture → thrombosis → myocardial infarction or unstable angina

Risk factors:
- Non-modifiable: Age (men ≥45, women ≥55), male sex, family history (first-degree relative with CAD <55M/<65F)
- Modifiable: Hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia, diabetes, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity, chronic stress

=== MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (HEART ATTACK) ===

Warning signs (may vary by individual):
- Crushing or squeezing chest pain/pressure (may radiate to left arm, jaw, neck, back)
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness
- Atypical in women/elderly: fatigue, jaw pain, epigastric pain, no chest pain

STEMI vs NSTEMI:
- STEMI: Complete coronary occlusion, ST-elevation on ECG → requires immediate reperfusion (PCI within 90 min)
- NSTEMI: Partial occlusion, troponin elevation without ST elevation

Emergency action: Call 911/112 immediately. Chew aspirin 325mg if not allergic. Do NOT drive self to hospital.

Treatment:
- Acute: Aspirin + antiplatelet (clopidogrel/ticagrelor), anticoagulation, primary PCI or thrombolytics
- Long-term: Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) 12 months, statin, ACE inhibitor/ARB, beta-blocker, cardiac rehab

=== HEART FAILURE ===

Definition: Heart cannot pump sufficient blood to meet body's needs
HFrEF (ejection fraction <40%): Systolic dysfunction — dilated, weak heart
HFpEF (ejection fraction ≥50%): Diastolic dysfunction — stiff heart, cannot fill properly

Symptoms: Dyspnoea on exertion → at rest → orthopnoea (lying flat), paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
Signs: Ankle oedema, raised JVP, crackles in lungs, S3 gallop
NYHA Classification: I (no symptoms) → II (symptoms on moderate exertion) → III (symptoms on mild exertion) → IV (symptoms at rest)

Treatment (HFrEF):
- Cornerstone: ACE inhibitor/ARB/ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan), beta-blocker (carvedilol/metoprolol), MRA (spironolactone), SGLT2 inhibitor
- Diuretics: For fluid overload (furosemide)
- Device therapy: ICD (sudden death prevention in EF <35%), CRT (dyssynchrony)

=== CHOLESTEROL & LIPIDS ===

Targets (ACC/AHA 2019):
- Very high risk (ASCVD or diabetes + risk factors): LDL < 55 mg/dL
- High risk (ASCVD risk >20%): LDL < 70 mg/dL
- Borderline risk: LDL < 100 mg/dL
- Low risk: LDL < 130 mg/dL

Optimal values:
- Total cholesterol: < 200 mg/dL
- HDL (good cholesterol): > 60 mg/dL (protective); < 40 mg/dL (men) / < 50 mg/dL (women) = risk factor
- LDL (bad cholesterol): Lower is better for high-risk patients
- Triglycerides: < 150 mg/dL; > 500 mg/dL = pancreatitis risk

Statins (first-line):
- High-intensity: atorvastatin 40–80mg, rosuvastatin 20–40mg (LDL reduction 50%)
- Moderate-intensity: atorvastatin 10–20mg, simvastatin 20–40mg (LDL reduction 30–50%)
- Side effects: myalgia (muscle pain), rarely rhabdomyolysis; check CK if severe muscle pain

Non-statin options: Ezetimibe (add-on), PCSK9 inhibitors (for very high risk, extremely high LDL)

Diet modifications:
- Reduce saturated fat (red meat, full-fat dairy) and trans fats (processed foods)
- Increase omega-3 fatty acids (fatty fish, walnuts, flaxseed)
- Soluble fibre (oats, barley, psyllium) reduces LDL by 5–10%
- Plant sterols/stanols (2g/day) reduce LDL by 10–15%

=== CARDIAC REHABILITATION ===
Structured programme post-MI or heart failure: supervised exercise, nutrition counselling, psychosocial support
Reduces re-admission by 30%, all-cause mortality by 20%
Recommended for all eligible post-MI and post-revascularisation patients
