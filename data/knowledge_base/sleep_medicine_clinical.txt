TOPIC: Sleep Medicine — Sleep Disorders and Sleep Hygiene
SOURCE: HealthBuddy Clinical Knowledge Base v3.0
CATEGORY: Sleep Medicine / Neurology

=== SLEEP PHYSIOLOGY ===
Normal sleep architecture cycles every 90 minutes:
- NREM Stage 1 (5%): Light sleep, hypnic jerks common
- NREM Stage 2 (50%): Sleep spindles, K-complexes; body temperature drops
- NREM Stage 3 (20%): Deep/slow-wave sleep; most restorative; growth hormone released
- REM (25%): Dreaming; memory consolidation; muscle atonia

Sleep needs by age:
- Newborns (0–3 months): 14–17 hours
- Toddlers (1–2 years): 11–14 hours
- School age (6–12 years): 9–12 hours
- Teenagers (13–18 years): 8–10 hours
- Adults (18–64 years): 7–9 hours
- Older adults (≥65 years): 7–8 hours

=== INSOMNIA ===
Prevalence: 30–35% experience insomnia symptoms; 10–15% chronic insomnia disorder

Types:
- Sleep onset insomnia: Difficulty falling asleep (>30 min to sleep onset)
- Sleep maintenance insomnia: Waking during night, difficulty returning to sleep
- Early morning awakening: Waking ≥30 min before intended time

Chronic insomnia: ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months, with daytime impairment

Causes:
- Primary: Hyperarousal (racing mind), conditioned arousal
- Secondary: Depression (early morning awakening classic), anxiety, chronic pain, sleep apnoea, medications (stimulants, SSRIs, steroids, beta-blockers), substance use

First-line treatment: CBT for Insomnia (CBT-I) — superior to medication long-term
CBT-I components:
1. Sleep restriction: Restrict time in bed to actual sleep time (initially may be 5–6h) → builds sleep pressure
2. Stimulus control: Bed only for sleep and sex; get up if not asleep in 20 min
3. Sleep hygiene (see below)
4. Relaxation techniques
5. Cognitive restructuring (challenging catastrophic thoughts about sleep)

Medications (short-term use only):
- Melatonin receptor agonists (ramelteon, melatonin): safest, best for circadian rhythm issues
- Z-drugs (zolpidem, zopiclone): risk of dependence, sleep-walking, next-day sedation; avoid in elderly
- Benzodiazepines: high dependence risk; not recommended for chronic use
- Doxepin (low dose): FDA-approved for sleep maintenance
- Antihistamines (diphenhydramine): OTC; rapid tolerance, anticholinergic effects — not recommended

=== OBSTRUCTIVE SLEEP APNOEA (OSA) ===
Upper airway collapses during sleep → apnoeic episodes → fragmented sleep, hypoxaemia

Risk factors: Obesity (BMI >30), male sex, age >40, large neck circumference (>40cm women, >43cm men), retrognathia, smoking, alcohol

Symptoms:
- Witnessed apnoeas / snoring (partner report important)
- Excessive daytime sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale ≥10 = significant)
- Non-restorative sleep, morning headaches, nocturia, cognitive impairment, mood changes
- In children: snoring, behavioural problems, hyperactivity, bedwetting

Diagnosis: Home sleep apnoea test (HSAT) or polysomnography (PSG)
AHI (Apnoea-Hypopnoea Index): Mild 5–15, Moderate 15–30, Severe >30 events/hour

Treatment:
- CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure): gold standard; adherence is key challenge (chin strap, heated humidifier help)
- Mandibular advancement device (MAD): for mild-moderate OSA, less effective than CPAP but better tolerated
- Weight loss: 10% weight reduction reduces AHI by ~26%
- Positional therapy: For positional OSA (AHI worse supine): tennis ball trick, positional alarms
- Surgery: UPPP, tonsillectomy (mainly in children), hypoglossal nerve stimulator (Inspire)
- Lifestyle: avoid alcohol/sedatives within 3h of sleep, side sleeping

Untreated OSA consequences: Hypertension, CAD, stroke, T2DM, cognitive impairment, motor vehicle accidents

=== CIRCADIAN RHYTHM DISORDERS ===

Delayed Sleep Phase Disorder (DSPD):
- "Night owl" extreme; unable to fall asleep before 2–4am; difficulty waking for conventional schedules
- Treatment: Morning bright light therapy (10,000 lux, 30 min), evening melatonin (0.5–1mg 5–6h before desired sleep time), gradual schedule advancement

Shift Work Sleep Disorder:
- Insomnia when trying to sleep, excessive sleepiness during waking hours
- Strategies: consistent sleep schedule on off-days, blackout curtains, modafinil (promotes wakefulness)

Jet Lag:
- East travel harder than west
- Recovery: ~1 day per time zone crossed
- Strategies: Adjust meal/sleep times to destination before travel; melatonin (0.5–5mg at destination bedtime); morning light exposure on arrival

=== SLEEP HYGIENE — EVIDENCE-BASED RECOMMENDATIONS ===
1. Consistent wake time: Most important anchor; wake at same time 7 days/week regardless of sleep quality
2. No screens 60 min before bed: Blue light suppresses melatonin production
3. Cool room: Optimal temperature 16–19°C (60–67°F) for sleep initiation
4. Dark room: Blackout curtains or sleep mask; even dim light impairs sleep quality
5. Quiet environment: Earplugs or white noise if noise is unavoidable
6. No caffeine after 2pm: Caffeine half-life 5–6 hours; affects sleep even if you can fall asleep
7. No alcohol: May help initiation but fragments second half of sleep, suppresses REM
8. No exercise within 2 hours of bed: Raises core temperature and adrenaline
9. Reserve bed for sleep: Do not work, watch TV, or scroll in bed
10. Avoid naps after 3pm or longer than 20–30 min

=== RESTLESS LEGS SYNDROME (RLS) ===
Irresistible urge to move legs, worse at rest, relieved by movement, worse in evening/night
Affects 5–10% of adults; often genetic; worsened by pregnancy, iron deficiency, renal failure

Treatment:
- Iron supplementation if ferritin <75 mcg/L
- Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) — first-line pharmacological
- Gabapentin/pregabalin — especially if pain component
- Avoid aggravating factors: antihistamines, antipsychotics, SSRIs, caffeine, alcohol
