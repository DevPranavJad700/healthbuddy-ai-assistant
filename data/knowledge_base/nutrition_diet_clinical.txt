=== MACRONUTRIENTS ===

Carbohydrates (45–65% of total calories):
- Simple vs Complex: Complex carbs (whole grains, legumes, vegetables) → slower glucose release, more fibre
- Glycaemic Index (GI): Low GI foods cause smaller blood sugar spikes (oats, legumes, most vegetables)
- Fibre: 25–38g/day recommended. Benefits: lowers cholesterol, improves glycaemic control, feeds gut microbiome
- Refined carbs (white bread, sugary drinks): associated with obesity, T2DM, metabolic syndrome

Protein (0.8 g/kg/day minimum; 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day for athletes or older adults):
- Complete proteins (all essential amino acids): meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy, quinoa
- Incomplete proteins (plant sources): beans, lentils, nuts — combine to get all amino acids
- Benefits: Satiety, muscle preservation, metabolic boost (thermic effect)
- Excess protein: No proven harm in healthy adults but monitor in CKD

Fats (20–35% of total calories):
- Unsaturated (healthy): olive oil, avocado, nuts, fatty fish (omega-3)
- Saturated (limit): red meat, butter, full-fat dairy → raises LDL
- Trans fats (eliminate): partially hydrogenated oils → raises LDL, lowers HDL, highly atherogenic
- Omega-3 fatty acids: EPA/DHA from fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) reduce triglycerides, inflammation; ALA from flaxseed/walnuts

=== DIETARY PATTERNS WITH STRONG EVIDENCE ===

Mediterranean Diet:
- High: olive oil, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, fish
- Moderate: poultry, eggs, dairy
- Low: red meat, sugar, processed foods
- Benefits: CV disease ↓29%, dementia risk ↓, T2DM ↓, all-cause mortality ↓
- PREDIMED trial: 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events vs low-fat diet

DASH Diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension):
- High: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein
- Low: sodium (<2,300mg/day), red meat, sweets, saturated fat
- Reduces SBP by 8–14 mmHg, effective for hypertension management

Plant-Based Diet:
- Well-planned vegan/vegetarian diets are nutritionally complete
- Monitor: Vitamin B12 (must supplement), iron, calcium, zinc, omega-3 (algae-based DHA), iodine
- Associated with lower BMI, lower CVD risk, lower T2DM risk

=== MICRONUTRIENTS — KEY DEFICIENCIES ===

Vitamin D:
- Deficiency extremely common (>1 billion worldwide)
- Symptoms of severe deficiency: bone pain, muscle weakness, frequent infections
- Sources: sunlight (15–30 min/day, UV index dependent), fatty fish, fortified foods
- Supplementation: 1,000–2,000 IU/day for most adults; test serum 25-OH-D to guide dosing
- Target: 40–60 ng/mL (100–150 nmol/L)

Vitamin B12:
- Deficiency in: vegans, elderly, metformin users, those with pernicious anaemia
- Symptoms: fatigue, peripheral neuropathy, megaloblastic anaemia, cognitive decline
- Food sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy; not found in plant foods
- Supplement: 25–100 mcg/day or 1000 mcg 3x/week

Iron:
- Iron deficiency anaemia: most common nutritional deficiency globally
- Symptoms: fatigue, pallor, pica, cold intolerance, brittle nails
- Sources: haem iron (red meat, poultry — well absorbed); non-haem (legumes, spinach — absorb better with Vitamin C)
- Avoid: tea/coffee with iron-rich meals (polyphenols inhibit absorption)
- Supplementation: 150–200 mg elemental iron/day in divided doses

Calcium:
- Recommended: 1,000 mg/day (adults), 1,200 mg/day (women >51, men >71)
- Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, tofu, broccoli, kale, sardines
- Calcium carbonate: take with food; calcium citrate: can take anytime
- Avoid high-dose supplements (>1,500 mg) — may increase cardiovascular risk

Folate (Vitamin B9):
- Critical in pregnancy: 400–800 mcg/day before conception and during first trimester (prevents neural tube defects)
- Sources: leafy greens, legumes, fortified cereals, liver

=== WEIGHT MANAGEMENT ===

BMI Classification:
- Underweight: < 18.5
- Normal: 18.5–24.9
- Overweight: 25–29.9
- Obesity Class I: 30–34.9; Class II: 35–39.9; Class III (Severe): ≥ 40
- Note: BMI does not account for muscle mass or fat distribution; waist circumference also important
  - High risk: Waist > 102cm (men), > 88cm (women)

Caloric deficit for weight loss:
- 500 kcal/day deficit → ~0.5 kg/week weight loss (safe and sustainable)
- Very low calorie diets (<800 kcal/day): medical supervision required
- Crash diets: cause muscle loss, metabolic adaptation, nutritional deficiencies

Evidence-based strategies:
1. Track food intake (calorie counting apps) — awareness drives behaviour change
2. Increase protein (satiety effect, preserves muscle during weight loss)
3. Reduce ultra-processed foods (engineered to override satiety signals)
4. Intermittent fasting (16:8 or 5:2): effective for weight loss, equivalent to daily calorie restriction
5. Mindful eating: eat slowly, without distraction; recognise hunger vs habit
6. Regular exercise: primarily benefits health (cardiovascular, mental), modest direct weight loss effect
7. Sleep: sleep deprivation raises ghrelin (hunger hormone), lowers leptin (satiety hormone)
8. Stress management: cortisol promotes fat storage (especially visceral)

Anti-obesity medications (when appropriate):
- Semaglutide (Wegovy): GLP-1 agonist, 15–20% body weight loss in trials — most effective currently
- Liraglutide (Saxenda): 5–10% body weight loss
- Orlistat: Blocks fat absorption — GI side effects; less effective
- Phentermine/topiramate: Appetite suppression — not for long-term use

=== FOOD SAFETY & HYDRATION ===

Hydration:
- General guideline: 8 cups (2L) water/day; more in heat, exercise, illness
- Better indicator: pale yellow urine = well hydrated; dark yellow = dehydrated
- Coffee/tea count toward fluid intake (mild diuretic effect negligible at moderate intake)

Food safety:
- Safe internal temperatures: poultry 74°C, ground meat 71°C, whole meat 63°C, fish 63°C
- Refrigerate perishables within 2 hours (1 hour if >32°C ambient)
- Danger zone: 4°C–60°C (bacteria multiply rapidly)
