=== DEVELOPMENTAL MILESTONES ===

2 months: Social smile, tracks objects, coos, lifts head briefly when prone
4 months: Laughs, bears weight on legs, reaches for objects
6 months: Sits with support, transfers objects, babbles consonants
9 months: Sits independently, crawls, pincer grasp developing, waves bye-bye
12 months: Pulls to stand, first words (mama, dada specifically), follows simple commands
18 months: Walks independently, 10+ words, uses spoon, points to body parts
2 years: 2-word phrases, runs, kicks ball, climbs stairs
3 years: 3-word sentences, pedals tricycle, dresses with help
5 years: Complete sentences, copies geometric shapes, counts to 10, fully toilet-trained

Red flag developmental delays requiring evaluation:
- No social smile by 3 months
- No babbling by 12 months
- No words by 16 months
- Loss of previously acquired language or social skills at any age
- Not walking by 18 months

=== VACCINATION SCHEDULE (UK / India Commonalities) ===

UK Routine Schedule (approximate):
- 8 weeks: 6-in-1 (DTaP/IPV/Hib/HepB), MenB, Rotavirus
- 12 weeks: 6-in-1 (dose 2), PCV (pneumococcal), Rotavirus (dose 2)
- 16 weeks: 6-in-1 (dose 3), MenB (dose 2)
- 1 year: MMR, MenB (dose 3), PCV (booster), Hib/MenC
- 2–6 years: Annual influenza (nasal spray)
- 12–13 years: HPV vaccine (2 doses)
- 14 years: Td/IPV (tetanus booster), MenACWY

India Universal Immunisation Programme:
- Birth: BCG, OPV dose 0, Hepatitis B
- 6 weeks: Pentavalent (DTP+Hib+HepB), IPV, OPV, Rotavirus, PCV
- 14 weeks: Pentavalent, IPV, OPV, Rotavirus, PCV
- 9–12 months: MR/MMR, JE vaccine (endemic areas), Vitamin A

Fever post-vaccination: Common, expected; treat with paracetamol; usually resolves in 24–48h
Anaphylaxis post-vaccination: Extremely rare (1–3 per million doses); remains in clinic for 15 min observation

=== COMMON CHILDHOOD ILLNESSES ===

Fever Management:
- Definition in children: Temperature >38°C (rectal) or >37.5°C (axillary)
- Infants <3 months with fever ≥38°C: Always seek medical evaluation (risk of serious bacterial infection)
- Infants 3–6 months with fever >38°C: Seek evaluation same day
- 6+ months: Assess well-being; paracetamol/ibuprofen for discomfort; seek care if fever >5 days, rash, very unwell

Febrile Seizures:
- Occur in 2–5% of children aged 6 months–5 years
- Simple: Generalised, <15 min, once in 24h, child recovers fully
- Management: Safety during seizure (do not restrain); call ambulance if >5 min; paracetamol for fever; reassure parents (rarely causes brain damage)
- Recurrence risk: 30%; increased in: age <18 months, close family history, fever <38.9°C at onset, short duration from fever onset

Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis):
- Viral (parainfluenza); barking seal-like cough, inspiratory stridor, hoarse voice; worst at night
- Mild: Cool mist air, calm child; single dose of dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg
- Moderate-severe: Nebulised adrenaline, observation; if severe stridor at rest or cyanosis → A&E

Bronchiolitis:
- RSV most common; peak winter; infants under 2 years
- Wheeze, tachypnoea, feeding difficulty, crackles
- Treatment: Supportive — maintain hydration, upright position, saline nasal drops; antibiotics NOT indicated
- Hospitalise if: SpO2 <92%, poor feeding (<50% of normal intake), breathing difficulty at rest

Hand Foot and Mouth Disease (HFMD):
- Coxsackie A16, Enterovirus 71; under 5 years
- Painful oral ulcers, vesicular rash on hands, feet, buttocks; mild fever
- Highly contagious; self-limiting 7–10 days; paracetamol, cold fluids, good hygiene

=== GROWTH AND NUTRITION IN CHILDREN ===

Breastfeeding:
- WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, continued with complementary foods to 2+ years
- Benefits: Optimal nutrition, immune protection (IgA), reduced SIDS risk, bonding, reduced obesity/T2DM in later life
- Formula: Complete nutrition alternative; follow preparation instructions exactly (hygiene critical)

Weaning / Complementary Feeding (from 6 months):
- Introduce single foods first; puree → mashed → soft lumps → family foods by 12 months
- Start: vegetables, fruits, cereals, meat/pulses; avoid honey (botulism risk) until 12 months; no salt/sugar added
- Iron-rich foods critical (6–12 months): meat, pulses, fortified cereals
- Allergens (peanuts, eggs, dairy, wheat): Introduce early (6–12 months) to reduce allergy risk
- Avoid: cow's milk as main drink until 12 months; no fruit juice; no added salt/sugar

Childhood obesity:
- BMI centile charts (not adult BMI) used in children
- Intervention: Family-based lifestyle modification; avoid labelling or shaming child
- Screen time: <1h/day 2–5 years; discourage for <18 months (except video calling)
- 60 min/day physical activity recommended

=== CHILD SAFETY AND INJURY PREVENTION ===

Safe sleep (infants, to prevent SIDS):
- Back to sleep always (supine)
- Firm flat mattress in own sleep space
- No loose bedding, pillows, bumpers in cot
- No smoking in home
- Room-sharing (not bed-sharing) for first 6 months
- Avoid overheating; head uncovered during sleep

Car seat safety: Rear-facing until 2 years (recommended); forward-facing to 4 years with harness; booster seat until 10–12 years or 57 inches tall

Choking prevention:
- Cut food into small pieces for under 4s
- Avoid: whole grapes, nuts, raw carrots, popcorn, hard candy in under 4s
- Choking child (conscious): 5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich); infants: 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts

Burns:
- Hot liquids most common in children; never leave hot drinks near babies
- Cool with cool (not cold) running water for 20 minutes; no butter or toothpaste

=== AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (ASD) ===
Neurodevelopmental condition; affects social communication and behaviour; wide spectrum

Early signs (12–24 months):
- Limited eye contact, no pointing by 12 months
- Not responding to name
- Limited pretend play
- Unusual sensory responses (over/under-sensitivity)
- Rigidity, repetitive behaviours

Diagnosis: Specialist assessment (paediatrician, SALT, psychologist); ADOS-2, ADI-R tools
No single blood test or scan diagnoses ASD

Management: Intensive early behavioural intervention (ABA, EIBI) most evidence-based
Speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, PECS for non-verbal children
Medications: For comorbidities (ADHD, anxiety, challenging behaviours) — not for core ASD
