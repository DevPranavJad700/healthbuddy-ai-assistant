=== MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER (MDD) ===

Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5): ≥5 of the following for ≥2 weeks, with at least one being depressed mood or anhedonia:
1. Depressed mood most of the day
2. Markedly diminished interest or pleasure in activities (anhedonia)
3. Significant weight change or appetite disturbance
4. Insomnia or hypersomnia
5. Psychomotor agitation or retardation (observable by others)
6. Fatigue or loss of energy
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
8. Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
9. Recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation

Severity: Mild (5 symptoms, minor impairment) → Moderate → Severe (with psychotic features)

Treatment:
- Mild-Moderate: Psychotherapy (CBT or IPT) ± SSRI/SNRI
- Moderate-Severe: Antidepressants + psychotherapy combined
- Antidepressants take 4–8 weeks for full effect; do not stop abruptly
- First-line SSRIs: sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine
- SNRIs: venlafaxine, duloxetine (especially useful when pain is comorbid)
- Psychotherapy: Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) — gold standard; restructures negative thought patterns
- Treatment-resistant: augmentation strategies, ketamine, TMS, ECT

When to seek emergency care: Active suicidal ideation with plan or intent → call emergency services or crisis line immediately

=== GENERALISED ANXIETY DISORDER (GAD) ===

Core features: Excessive, uncontrollable worry about multiple domains (health, finances, relationships) for ≥6 months, with ≥3 somatic symptoms (restlessness, fatigue, concentration difficulty, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance)

Physical symptoms: Palpitations, sweating, trembling, GI upset, frequent headaches

Treatment:
- First-line: CBT (worry exposure + cognitive restructuring)
- SSRIs/SNRIs (escitalopram, venlafaxine) — takes 2–4 weeks
- Buspirone: non-addictive anxiolytic, good for long-term use
- Benzodiazepines: only short-term (risk of dependence)
- Lifestyle: regular exercise reduces anxiety by ~40% in controlled trials

=== PANIC DISORDER ===

Discrete episodes of intense fear peaking within 10 minutes:
- Heart pounding, sweating, trembling
- Shortness of breath, chest pain
- Fear of dying or losing control
- Depersonalisation/derealisation

Rule out cardiac causes first. Panic attack ≠ heart attack (though symptoms overlap).

Treatment: CBT (interoceptive exposure), SSRIs, avoid caffeine and alcohol

=== STRESS MANAGEMENT — EVIDENCE-BASED STRATEGIES ===

1. Diaphragmatic Breathing (4-7-8 technique):
   Inhale 4 sec, hold 7 sec, exhale 8 sec. Activates parasympathetic system, reduces cortisol.

2. Progressive Muscle Relaxation:
   Tense and release muscle groups sequentially. 20 minutes daily reduces anxiety.

3. Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR):
   8-week structured programme. Reduces anxiety by 58% (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2014).

4. Exercise:
   150 min/week aerobic exercise reduces depression symptoms as effectively as antidepressants in mild-moderate cases (Blumenthal et al.)

5. Sleep Hygiene:
   Consistent schedule, dark cool room, no screens 1 hour before bed, avoid caffeine after 2pm

6. Social Support:
   Social connection is one of the strongest protective factors against mental illness.

7. Journaling:
   Expressive writing (15–20 min, 3 consecutive days) reduces intrusive thoughts.

=== STIGMA & SEEKING HELP ===
Mental illness is a medical condition, not a character weakness. Seeking help is a sign of strength. Depression and anxiety are among the most treatable conditions in medicine — 80%+ respond to treatment.
